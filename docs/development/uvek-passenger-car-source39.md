# Source-3.9 car supplies and rare-earth allocation proxies

The isolated November 10 car archive's source-3.9 cohort needed eight
correspondences. The subsequent full workflow downloaded the newer November 16
archive and stopped first on 21 source-3.10 correspondences. That current archive
and the complete car run remain unfinished; the results below establish the
reviewed source-3.9 mappings and focused conversion checks.

Six use
complete native supplies: EAF low-alloy steel, chromium steel 18/8, graphite,
boric oxide, capacitor-grade tantalum powder and underground hazardous-waste
disposal. References and product function were checked against both native
releases. Two existing steel profile guards gain a source-3.9 entry; the other
four migration rules remain unchanged. Waste demand keeps its existing sign
conversion. Product grade, primary/secondary mix, freight, weak production data
and native emissions exclusions remain explicit limitations.

The two remaining candidates were chemically incorrect: holmium and terbium
oxides pointed to lithium manganese oxide. No permission is given for those
matches. Instead, two clearly labelled allocation-proxy markets retain the actual
named oxide outputs.

The source oxides come from a joint rare-earth-carbonate process. In both source
regions, their full nonzero input vectors are proportional to neodymium-oxide
production from that same joint process. The comparison checks supplier/flow
UUID, exchange type and unit: 136 groups in RoW and 38 in CN-FJ for each oxide,
348 groups in total. The observed reference-process demand is approximately
0.0543394 for terbium oxide and 5.48788 for holmium oxide per source kg.
These are allocation ratios read from the source inventories. They are not
chemical, molar-mass or market-price conversion factors, and the review does not
independently validate the source allocation prices.

The new markets use those ratios to demand the complete native neodymium-oxide
processing activity as a burden reference. This does not describe neodymium
as material feed or claim conversion of one element into another. Both original
producer entries remain separate after the explicit reference-process scaling.
All four rail, barge, road and container-ship freight entries retain their original
quantities and marginal uncertainty, without aggregation or renormalization.
There is no extra factory helper, treatment credit or source-exchange omission.

The native production route uses Chinese bastnasite; the source producers refine
ion-adsorption-clay carbonates. This is a substantial ore and technology proxy.
Source geographic differences, element-specific resource depletion, separation,
purity, allocation and emissions are not reproduced by the native reference
inventory. Their score effects are unquantified. The resulting inventories are
screening estimates and do not establish scientific or LCIA equivalence.

Seven focused migration/replay tests pass. They check signed stock and waste
demands, uncertainty, actual oxide outputs, separate producer entries, freight
quantities, old profile rules and atomic replay. Clean generation reproduces all
three forward resources byte for byte. Runtime Python and the public sector gate
remain unchanged; car-sector qualification still requires the actual shared run
and its physical, combined-scenario and finite-LCI checks.
