# Allocated oil CHP heat with carbon capture

UVEK has no established direct oil CHP heat supplier. This authored screening reconstruction instead uses complete German native heavy-fuel-oil power-plant combustion, scaled to preserve the inspected ecoinvent 3.6 cut-off oil CHP heat's allocated oil demand. The derived coefficient is 0.3829528198799506 MJ combustion service per MJ useful heat. It reproduces both separately reported source CO2 components. This preserves the source allocation basis; it does not infer physical efficiency, substitute an unallocated furnace, or add a coproduct electricity credit.

Retain the original heat CCS model's 9% heat-input penalty. The native combustion service reports 0.078 kg fossil CO2/MJ from combustion and separately 0.00061791 kg/MJ from limestone flue-gas treatment. The original CCS convention captures only combustion CO2. Accordingly, capture demand and the negative urban-air fossil counter-exchange are both 0.02930278387157407 kg/MJ heat. Limestone CO2 remains positive and uncaptured: 0.0002579271108559022 kg/MJ after the heat-input penalty. Ninety percent refers to combustion CO2, not total emissions including limestone treatment or upstream capture-chain burdens.

The complete native power-plant service retains fuel production, distribution, infrastructure, treatment chemicals, water, emissions and residues. A fixed German allocated-heat reference prevents regional relinking from changing the allocation basis while preserving its capture credit. The model reproduces the inspected source oil draw and CO2 components, not all source inputs or an equivalent LCIA score. Other burdens use the complete native inventory and retain its geographic, technology, vintage, uncertainty and background limitations.

As in the existing oil heat CCS workbook, the complete hard-coal post-combustion capture service is a screening proxy for oil capture. It retains capture energy, infrastructure, 200 km pipeline, storage at 1,000 m and leakage. Oil-specific flue-gas chemistry, reagent demand, energy performance and plant scale remain unqualified; no unsupported performance multiplier or second recovery credit is added. The original 3.5 workbook's rounded credit does not replace the inspected 3.6 allocation witness.

The mapping requires active IAM role data and uses normal lazy CLIC preparation and the shared heat transformation. No Python runtime module changes or alternative UVEK heat engine are introduced. Controlled qualification does not establish a complete real TIAM or combined-fuels scenario.

## Controlled verification

The normal update, exhaustive validation and Brightway writer produced 12,074 activities in a controlled 16-region TIAM-shaped case: 16 oil CCS heat suppliers, 17 secondary heat markets and ten complete CLIC capture-chain records. Ninety signed-demand LCIs were finite. Exported native oil power-plant exchange quantities and provider codes matched the original exactly. An independent exported-graph comparison reproduced the inspected source heat allocated oil mass and both CO2 components within relative tolerance 1e-12. The shared heat/mapping/preparation suite passed 108 tests. See `uvek-native-oil-ccs-heat-verification.json` for scope and evidence hashes.

The derived allocation coefficient is fixed in this screening model. Source process internal uncertainty is replaced by the native inventory uncertainty, rather than reconstructed from the fuel ratio. Caller quantities and supported caller uncertainty still follow normal Premise handling. No full inventory or LCIA equivalence is established.
