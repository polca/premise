# Diesel exhaust in machinery operating services

The current fuel workflow exposed a real gap: 48 native excavation, farming and forestry services already bought the IAM diesel blend, but their direct exhaust remained labelled entirely fossil. They include irrigation, tillage, harvesting, excavation and wood chipping. This affects operating services that the new blend actually supplies; it does not require another upstream reconstruction.

Five exact selector groups were added to the existing diesel YAML mapping, covering the native hectare, cubic-metre, hour, unit and kilogram references. The existing shared callback preserves the recorded positive CO2 total and updates its fossil/non-fossil split from the linked fuel. No production Python module, fuel quantity, efficiency, infrastructure, process allocation or migration resource changes. Six regression cases cover these reference bases, reject wrong names/products/units and verify repeated updates, signed credits and uncertain fuel inputs.

The complete native input inventories were inspected. Two forestry harvesters also buy rapeseed oil without a sufficiently documented carbon boundary; they are excluded and the actual run confirms they remain unchanged. The added services use the previously approved fuel-dominant exhaust convention: unidentified minor lubricant carbon can also be reclassified. Lubricant supply and disposal remain separate and unchanged. No guessed carbon deduction or avoided-emission credit is added. This approximation has an unquantified score effect.

The actual encrypted REMIND SSP2-NPi 2050 fuel workflow passes exhaustive graph validation. Independent before/after comparisons verify unchanged total positive CO2 and every other exchange for all 48 services. All 981 resolved consumers selected by the complete existing exhaust mappings have the expected origin split. This count does not include unresolved-origin consumers or certify all industrial carbon emissions.

All 219 relevant regressions pass. Six signed LCIs on the complete 13,122-activity transformed graph are finite and reverse consistently: Swiss excavation, US irrigation per cubic metre, and European mobile wood chipping. No Brightway database or activity is written. Black, Ruff and the documentation-reference check pass. The temporary matrix snapshot was removed after the checks.

The broader link audit also explains why a simple “does not reach the new market” count overstates the problem. Native production gas, flaring/venting, LNG transport chains and fossil refinery components are distinct upstream boundaries. Fixed 4% and 15% ETBE grades retain their recipe. Five rare-earth processing inventories buy refinery kerosene and document solvent extraction; their kerosene is not established as a delivered combustion fuel and was not redirected to an average fuel mix. These observations justify preserving those links, not declaring every possible fuel boundary complete.

There are still 236 native positive-CO2 review candidates outside the current exact exhaust selectors. This is a screening list, not 236 confirmed errors: it includes delivery carriers, industrial process/feedstock carbon, mixed-fuel services and legacy operating services. Further work must distinguish these boundaries, prioritizing real missed consumers over recursive upstream mapping. Public fuel support and export qualification remain unfinished.
