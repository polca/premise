# Fuel update through the normal BAFU workflow

The standard public workflow now runs nine sectors together for BAFU/UVEK 2026 cut-off with the ecoinvent 3.10 biosphere: biomass, electricity, heat, cement, steel, mining, renewable, cdr and fuels. The test uses encrypted REMIND SSP2-NPi 2050 and normal checkpoint persistence. There is no support-guard bypass, alternative sector engine or validation bypass. The no-argument default remains biomass, electricity and heat.

The normal Brightway export contains 16,117 activities and retains all 11,947 native source codes. The source database metadata is unchanged. Every one of its 157,336 technosphere links points inside the exported database; all 322,215 biosphere exchanges point to the selected ecoinvent 3.10 biosphere. The output is `BAFU:2026-REMIND-SSP2-NPi-2050-nine-fuels-20261115-124321` in the existing project `bafu-2026-ecospold2-biosphere-310`.

The written database contains 78 principal fuel markets: petrol, diesel, kerosene, high- and low-pressure natural gas, and hydrogen, each in all thirteen REMIND regions including EUR, NEU and World. All 22 fuel routes with positive IAM production appear as positive components of these written markets. EUR/NEU electricity market groups, buildings-heat markets and carbon-removal markets are also present. No unresolved market carbon-origin record remains in this run.

Written EUR PEM hydrogen consumes 45.3 kWh/kg, agreeing with the corrected efficiency floor. The native reformer's initial gas input is recognized as 4.37 cubic-metres equivalent, while its physical exchanges retain their original unit. The twelve regional hydrogen markets each add one separate distribution input; World aggregates regional supply and adds none directly. Delivered PEM hydrogen is excluded from the production mix, while remaining available for heat-input classification. The combined run independently confirms valid native hydrogen-boiler heat factors after that YAML-only classification correction.

All 176 written positive/negative unit-demand LCIs are finite and reverse consistently. They cover all 78 principal fuel markets and ten representative producers or operating services, including both mixed petrol/diesel fleets, excavation, irrigation, wood chipping, waste collection and helicopter flight/LTO. Inspection creates no additional database or activity. Only one new database was written for this export milestone; exploratory checks used in-memory matrices and their temporary snapshots were removed.

Offline checks pass: 337 fuel/background regressions and 147 cross-sector/validation regressions. The separate 189-case heat/classification suite overlaps those tests. The maintainer registry test needs `dev` on `PYTHONPATH`; no source-code change was needed for that test environment. Black, Ruff and documentation-reference checks pass. Full licensed ecoinvent process tests were not run; these results distinguish synthetic/shared regressions from the real installed BAFU workflow.

The additional production-Python change for public qualification is limited to the supported-sector list and its error message. The substantive fixes are the documented YAML carrier/supply distinctions, exact operating-exhaust selectors, and the separately reviewed small generic hydrogen unit/floor corrections. Existing public guard tests now exercise an actually unsupported sector rather than a sector already qualified. Colleagues can use the same constructor and update interface, selecting the nine sectors above in one `ndb.update([...])` call.

This is operational qualification for the inspected scenario, not a claim that every inherited source inventory has a complete future carbon model. Broad industrial or feedstock CO2 pools, source-grade recipes, two-stroke fuel and mixed auxiliary-carbon boundaries are not assigned wholly to a purchased fuel. Native gas loss-flow origins and existing proxy, allocation, vintage and biosphere-exclusion limitations remain explicit. Those limitations do not authorize silently replacing a physically different process or dropping an unresolved exchange. No LCIA-equivalence or uncertainty-simulation claim is made.

Nine of nineteen sectors are now qualified. Metals, battery, cars, two_wheelers, trucks, ships, buses, trains, final energy and emissions remain unfinished. The full integration goal remains active. The next work prioritizes bounded sector blockers and shared transport/material gaps, rather than automatically reconstructing trace upstream battery dependencies. The separate metals failure has been identified as a retired generic sodium-to-water identity; it is not established as an exact ionic-sodium synonym and no correspondence has been invented.
