# Allocated steelmaking and rolling heat

Three ecoinvent 3.9 cut-off heat co-products now use a process-burden proxy instead of the previous gas-heat suggestions. These are dependencies of the industrial other-fuel heat mix, which remains unapproved.

The complete nominal technosphere and biosphere inventory of each source heat co-product is proportional to its own main-product inventory. The two Austrian/rest-of-world electric-arc steelmaking references use 0.02264957265 kg of allocated steelmaking burden per MJ. Austrian hot rolling uses 0.15384615385 kg of rolling-service burden per MJ. This is allocation accounting, not a physical conversion of heat into metal, a steel purchase, an avoided-production credit or a claim about heat-recovery efficiency.

The native European scrap-based electric-arc steelmaking and casting process provides the first two proxies. It is an un-/low-alloyed European fleet proxy for the source low-alloyed reinforcing-billet technology. No converter, mixed-steel market, rolled reinforcing-steel product or worst-plant dataset is selected. Native European hot rolling is a processing service excluding steel. It replaces integrated hot-billet rolling with a process having different reheating and natural-gas furnace assumptions; its energy and allocation differences are explicit limitations. Each complete native process is counted once. No boiler, network, loss multiplier or extra steel input is added.

Geography, product scope, technology, allocation, vintage and internal uncertainty differ; score effects are unquantified. Caller signs, formulas and supported uncertainty are scaled consistently. Nominal inventory proportionality does not establish identical stochastic correlations between source co-products. These exact positive-reference 3.9 cut-off heat identities alone qualify. Pulp's carbon resource corrections and nonzero internal incinerator reuse heat are not included. Other releases, consequential and reverse routes remain unapproved. No helper datasets or runtime Python module changes are introduced.
