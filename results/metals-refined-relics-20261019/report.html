This is a consistency check of the resource-attribution convention. Many direct resource exchanges are set to one by premise, so agreement is not independent validation of ore grades, physical recoveries, or allocation. Values above one may include recovery losses and upstream extraction for other inputs; values below one require checking attribution and upstream supply mixtures.

Interpretation of departures

  • Zinc-refining co-products: copper 0.01354, lead 0.0001617, and silver 0.0001706 kg/kg. The upstream-presence check finds small inputs of the same metal and prevents adding the primary resource attribution. The corresponding matching feed masses are only 0.000245, 0.01311, and 0.000167 kg/kg respectively; even their total mass cannot explain a one-kg output.
  • Lithium and arsenic: elemental lithium is about 1.8×10⁻¹¹ kg/kg and 7N arsenic about 2.55×10⁻⁷ kg/kg. These final producers are not selected by the mining-share attribution mapping. Their low scores expose coverage/attribution gaps; they do not demonstrate negligible physical extraction.
  • Silicon: 0.00153–0.00305 kg/kg. Metallurgical grade is not recognized as a pure target product by the correction, and RELICS characterizes elemental Silicon rather than converting the silica/sand extraction feeding this process.
  • Copper electrorefining: 0.90462 kg/kg. Unresolved copper concentrates retain their source resource amounts, which can still reflect economic allocation. For example, the copper-concentrate co-product of zinc mining retains about 0.0121 kg direct Cu resource/kg concentrate. This fallback is not a verified composition/recovery correction.
  • Values above one: 7N gallium consumes 1.42857 kg high-grade gallium/kg output, explaining its score of 1.42857. Aluminium, iron and nickel departures also largely persist from the original upstream inventories. A score above one is not automatically an error: recovery losses and upstream inputs can require more extracted metal than the final product contains.
